# The chemistry of oxygen
Due to its in water formation and its abundance, oxygen evolution over the life of molecular cloud is the key to understand. Oxygen bearing species can be divided into two categories:
1. those which maximum abundance in gas phase  is reached before getting to the cloud's steady state ($\sim$ 10$^5$-10$^6$ years), *e.g.* $\ce{H2O}$
2. those wich maximum abundance is reached at the steady state, as $\ce{CO}$ and $\ce{O2}$. 

The great majority of atomic oxygen is trapped in $\ce{CO}$ with a relative abundance  of 10$^{-4}$ relative to $\ce{H2}$ reached around 10$^5$ years{{< cite "wakelamrev">}} after the cloud formation. 

Molecular oxygen enters in the $\ce{H2O}$ formation through the $\ce{H2O2}$ and $\ce{O3}$ reaction networks {{< cite "Ioppolo2008">}}. This is shown by the strong correlation between $\ce{H2O}$ and $\ce{O2}$ abundance  found in the 67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko cometary coma{{< cite "bieler2015abundant">}}. 

$\ce{O2}$ was  detected in the warm region of the $\rho$-Ophiuchi cloud{{< cite "o2obs2007">}},{{< cite "o2obs2012">}} with an abundance of $5 \times 10^{-8}$ relative to $\ce{H2}$. The same abundance is estimated for cold molecular clouds{{< cite "o2estimate2003">}}. 

The difficulty in observing $\ce{O2}$ in interstellar clouds is due to  its infrared inactivity. The molecule can be observed in its vibrational mode ($\nu=1551$ cm$^{-1}$) only when it is embedded in a matrix.{{< cite "caselli2018">}}

The mismatch between predictions and observations can be explained by the adsorption of  $\ce{O2}$ on tsurfaces. This hypothesis is  supported by the evidence of intermediate species involved in water formation network{{< cite "cuppenioppolo2010-2">}} ($\ce{HO2}$ and $\ce{H2O2}$) in the $\rho$-Ophiuchi cloud. Other possible causes such as very young or carbon rich clouds are ruled out due to their specific conditions{{< cite "Markwick_2000">}}.

## Atomic oxygen surface diffusion 
An important factor for the oxygen atom reactivity is its diffusion mechanism. Diffusion can only occur on the top of the surface as crossing the first water bulk layer would take 10$^{29}$ {{<cite "minissale2019">}}. The diffusion energy ($E_{\textrm{dif}}$) is estimated by temperature desorption experiments from the  desorption energy ($E_{\textrm{des}}$):
$$ E_{\textrm{dif}}= 0.7 E_{\textrm{des}}$$
From $E_{\textrm{des}} \sim 1320$ K, it derived that $E_{\textrm{dif}} \sim 750$ K.{{< cite "minissale2016">}}. 
Temperature desorption experiments from 6-25 K show that the measured diffusion does not follow a typical Arrhenius form:
$$
    D = D_0 \exp \left( \frac{ E_\textrm{dif}}{k_BT}\right)
$$
with $D_0$ as Arrhenius constant, and $k_{\textrm{B}}$ the Boltzmann constant, opening the question of what could be the reason of this deviation.

## Oxygen production
In gas phase, $\ce{O2}$ is manly produced by the reaction:{{< cite "davidsson1990,carty2006">}} 

$$\ce{OH + O -> O2 + H}$$

while on top of the surface, the two reactions:
$$\ce{O +O-> O2}$$

and  
$$ \ce{HO2 + H -> O2 + H2 }$$
are competing,  with the first one{{< cite "ioppolo2011">}} more efficient than the  second, due to the presence of a high activation barrier (19.0 kcal/mol).{{< cite "Mousavipour2007">}}  Oxygen formation through decomposition of $\ce{H2O2}$ does not seem to be an efficient mechanism, due to its lower efficiency.{{< cite "dulieu2017">}}.


The recombination of two oxygen atoms in their ground states ($^3$P) generates vibrational excited $\ce{O2}$ in three possible electronic states (X$^3\Sigma_g^-$, a$^1\Delta_g$, b$^1\Sigma_g^+$). The a$^1\Delta_g$ and b$^1\Sigma_g^+$  excited states are known for their higher reactivity compared to the X$^3\Sigma_g^-$ ground state. 

 The electric dipole transition $a^1\Delta_g \rightarrow$ $X^3\Sigma_g^-$ and $b^1 \Sigma_g^+ \rightarrow$ $X^3\Sigma_g^-$ are forbidden by selection rules. The former is both spin- and symmetry-forbidden, whereas the latter is only spin-forbidden. Nevertheless, these transitions can occur through magnetic-dipole and electric-quadrupole interactions, leading to long radiative lifetimes of approximately 4000 s and 150 s, respectively.{{< cite "parker:2014" >}}

 However, forbidden transitions occurs when the environment breaks the molecular symmetry. Relaxation from the $b^1\Sigma_g^+$ state  to $X^3\Sigma_g^-$  occurs *via* Inter System Crossing (ISC), governed by spin orbit coupling (SOC). This process can be described using the Landau Zener formalism. {{< cite "wodtke:1999,minaev2003,dayou2005">}}.
